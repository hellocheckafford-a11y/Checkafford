What happens if you miss a BNPL payment?

Missing a Buy Now Pay Later payment can cost you more than you think — from late fees to credit score damage. Here is exactly what happens on each platform.

Buy Now Pay Later has exploded in popularity. But what most users do not fully understand is what happens when a payment is missed — and it varies significantly by platform.

What to Do If You Cannot Make a Payment

  1. Contact the provider before the due date — most have hardship programs they do not advertise
  2. Request a payment reschedule — Klarna and Affirm allow date changes, sometimes with no penalty
  3. Pay the minimum if you cannot pay in full — even partial payment sometimes prevents delinquency
  4. Do not take new BNPL to cover old ones — this is a debt spiral

What Counts as a Missed Payment

Most BNPL providers charge each installment automatically from your card or bank account on the due date. If that payment fails, because of low funds or an expired card, the plan is usually treated as late. Many providers give a short grace period or retry the payment, but the rules differ by provider and by plan, so read the terms of each purchase.

What Usually Happens, Step by Step

  1. The payment fails, and the provider may retry it or send a reminder
  2. A late fee may be added, depending on the provider and the plan
  3. Your account may be paused, which means you cannot start new BNPL purchases until you catch up
  4. If the debt stays unpaid, the provider may send it to collections, and collections can appear on your credit report

How BNPL Can Affect Your Credit

Reporting is changing. Some providers now report their loans to credit bureaus, and FICO has announced scoring models that can take BNPL data into account. That means on-time BNPL payments may start to help, and missed ones may start to hurt, even when a provider does not report every plan today. Check your credit reports to see what is listed, and read each provider's current policy.

Why Several Plans at Once Gets Risky

BNPL feels small because each payment is small. But if you have four or five plans running at once, across several providers, the due dates and amounts add up, and it is easy to miss one. A reasonable guardrail is to keep all active installments under about 5% of your monthly take-home pay.

Example: $3,500 Take-Home Pay

5% guardrail: $175 per month across all BNPL plans · Four plans of $60 each = $240 per month, already above the guardrail

If You Already Missed a Payment

  1. Pay the overdue installment as soon as you can, because fees and collection risk grow with time
  2. Contact the provider; many have hardship options such as moving the due date or setting up a new schedule
  3. Turn on reminders and keep a cushion in the account linked to autopay
  4. Review your credit reports for any BNPL entries and dispute anything that looks wrong
  5. Pause new BNPL purchases until your current plans are paid off
