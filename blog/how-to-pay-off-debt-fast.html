How to pay off $10,000 in debt fast

Ten thousand dollars in debt feels overwhelming. But with the right method and a clear monthly target, most people can eliminate it in 12 to 24 months.

By Elba Nabetcy Guissepe Avalo, actuary
Updated October 2026
4 min read

$10,000 in debt is one of the most common financial situations in America. Whether it is credit cards, a personal loan, or a mix — the path out is the same. What changes is how fast you travel it.

Step 1: Know Exactly What You Owe

Your Debt Inventory

Write down every debt: Creditor | Balance | Interest Rate (APR) | Minimum Payment · Example: Chase Visa $3,200 at 24.99% · Discover $4,100 at 19.99% · Personal Loan $2,700 at 11.5% — Total: $10,000 | Min payments: ~$232/mo

Step 2: Choose Your Method

Avalanche Method — pay minimums on everything, put extra toward the highest interest rate first. Saves the most money. Best for disciplined people motivated by math.

Snowball Method — pay minimums on everything, put extra toward the smallest balance first. Fastest psychological wins. Research shows higher completion rates.

Avalanche vs. Snowball: A Real Comparison

Using the example debts, assume minimum payments of $90, $82, and $60, and a total of $600 per month going toward debt.

MethodPayoff OrderTimeTotal Interest
AvalancheChase, Discover, Personal loanAbout 20 months~$1,525
SnowballPersonal loan, Chase, DiscoverAbout 20 months~$1,880

The avalanche method saves about $360 here, and both methods finish at about the same time. The best method is the one you will actually stick with. If closing an account quickly keeps you motivated, the snowball costs little extra.

Where to Find Extra Money for Debt

  • Review three months of bank statements and cancel subscriptions you do not use
  • Sell items you no longer need and put the full amount toward your highest-interest debt
  • Pick up temporary extra income, such as overtime or a weekend job, with a fixed end date
  • Put tax refunds and bonuses toward debt before you spend them
  • Call your card issuer and ask for a lower rate; some will agree, especially after on-time payments

Balance Transfers and Consolidation Loans

A balance transfer card offers a low or 0% introductory rate for a limited time, usually in exchange for a transfer fee of a few percent. It helps only if you can pay off the balance before the promotion ends. A consolidation loan replaces several debts with one fixed payment, and its rate depends on your credit. Always compare the total cost, including fees, against what you would pay by attacking the debt directly.

Mistakes That Slow You Down

  • Paying only the minimums and wondering why the balance barely moves
  • Running the cards up again after paying them down
  • Skipping a starter emergency fund, so a surprise bill goes straight back on a card
  • Closing every paid-off account without checking how it affects your credit

When to Get Professional Help

If your payments are already more than you can afford, a nonprofit credit counseling agency can review your budget and, in some cases, set up a debt management plan. Look for agencies affiliated with the National Foundation for Credit Counseling, and avoid any company that charges large upfront fees or promises to erase your debt.
